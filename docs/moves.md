# What each move teaches

Every move in `src/moves/defs/` carries a `teaches` line: one sentence, at most 90 characters, on what the move is for in a real situation. The lines are about protecting yourself and getting away, not about hurting anyone. `tests/teaches.test.ts` fails if a move has no line, a line that is too long, or a line that differs from its row in the table below.

Each line is our own wording, checked against the source next to it. The sources were opened and read on 14 November 2026; their full titles and URLs are listed under the table.

| Move | `teaches` | Source checked | What the source says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block (`block`) | Hands high protects your head while you look for a way out | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 67 "Guard"; [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Roof Block" | A sound guard protects the target area, and hands held too low leave the chin open. A raised forearm block protects the head. |
| Jab (`jab`) | A fast hand straight out in front holds someone at arm's length so they cannot crowd you | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 73 "The straight lead hand" | The straight punch with the front hand measures distance and is a key part of defence: it holds up someone rushing in and stops them getting settled. |
| Cross (`cross`) | Strength comes from your feet and hips, not your arm, so stay balanced and ready to move | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 72 "Principles of punching" and p. 75 "The straight rear hand" | The body drives the punch from the floor through the hips. A good base and balance are essential, and an "arm punch" is listed as an error. |
| Hook (`hook`) | Swings come from the side, out of view, so always bring your hand back to your face | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 77 "The lead hand hook" and p. 67 "Guard" | A hook arrives from outside the defender's field of vision. The hand returns to guard afterwards, and hands held away from the face leave an opening for a hook. |
| Duck (`duck`) | Bend your knees, not your waist, to drop under a swing and still see what is coming | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 84 "The duck" | Bend at the knees, not the waist, with hands up and eyes on the other person the whole time. |
| Dodge left (`dodge_left`) | Step off the line of a rush: a moving target is hard to reach and free to get away | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 79 "Principles of defence"; [Safety Tips][mu] | Someone who keeps moving is far harder to land a punch on, and the feet are what carry a boxer in and out of range. When a way out opens, run to a safe place with people in it. |
| Dodge right (`dodge_right`) | Moving sideways, not straight back, takes you out of an attack's path so you can get away | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 79 "The importance of lateral movement"; [Staying Safe][hpd], "When Physical Defense is Necessary" | Defences built on footwork should step sideways off the path of the attack instead of always moving in straight lines. Getting away, not winning a fight, is the aim. |
| Overhead strike (`overhead_strike`) | An arm raised over your head shields you from a blow coming down from above | [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Roof Block" | A forearm lifted level with the forehead covers the head against a strike from the front or from overhead. |
| Spear thrust (`spear_thrust`) | Two open hands out front keep someone at arm's length while you back away | [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Safety Stance"; [Safety Tips][mu] | Hands up with open palms, at least an arm's length from the other person, backing away. Keep your distance and tell the person to get back. |
| Stake thrust (`stake_thrust`) | Use your body weight, not just arm strength, to break a grip and get free | [Self Defense handout][asu], "Wrist Hold" and "Leverage your weight"; [Staying Safe][hpd], "When Physical Defense is Necessary" | Drop into a strong stance and lean in to break a hold; using your weight works whatever your size. Shift your weight to break free of a grab. |

## Notes

- Sources 2, 4 and 5 are written for people already under attack, so they also describe strikes and the parts of the body to aim at. None of that is used here. We took only their points about keeping distance, covering the head, shifting body weight and getting away, and wrote them in our own words.
- The spear thrust, overhead strike and stake thrust are stage moves with a spear and a stake. Their lines name the nearest real idea: hands out to hold distance, an arm raised to cover the head, and body weight against a grip. They do not claim the game's motion is a taught technique.
- This is a game, not a self-defense class. The lines point at ideas a real course teaches with a partner and an instructor.
