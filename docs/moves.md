# What each move teaches

> **Safety note.** This game is practice for two things: reacting in time and keeping your distance. It is no replacement for a class with an instructor and a partner. In a real situation the first choice is always to leave: get to a safe place with people in it. Nothing here is a reason to stay and fight.

Every move in `src/moves/defs/` carries a `teaches` line: one sentence, at most 90 characters, on what the move is for in a real situation. The lines are about protecting yourself and getting away, not about hurting anyone. `tests/teaches.test.ts` fails if a move has no line, a line that is too long, or a line that differs from its row in the table below.

Each line is our own wording, checked against the source next to it. The sources were opened and read on 14 November 2026; their full titles and URLs are listed under the table.

| Move | `teaches` | Source checked | What the source says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block (`block`) | Hands high protects your head while you look for a way out | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 67 "Guard"; [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Roof Block" | A sound guard protects the target area, and hands held too low leave the chin open. A raised forearm block protects the head. |
| Jab (`jab`) | A fast hand straight out in front holds someone at arm's length so they cannot crowd you | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 73 "The straight lead hand" | The straight punch with the front hand measures distance and is a key part of defence: it holds up someone rushing in and stops them getting settled. |
| Cross (`cross`) | Strength comes from your feet and hips, not your arm, so stay balanced and ready to move | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 72 "Principles of punching" and p. 75 "The straight rear hand" | The body drives the punch from the floor through the hips. A good base and balance are essential, and an "arm punch" is listed as an error. |
| Hook (`hook`) | Swings come from the side, out of view, so always bring your hand back to your face | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 77 "The lead hand hook" and p. 67 "Guard" | A hook arrives from outside the defender's field of vision. The hand returns to guard afterwards, and hands held away from the face leave an opening for a hook. |
| Duck (`duck`) | Bend your knees, not your waist, to drop under a swing and still see what is coming | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 84 "The duck" | Bend at the knees, not the waist, with hands up and eyes on the other person the whole time. |
| Dodge left (`dodge_left`) | Step off the line of a rush: a moving target is hard to reach and free to get away | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 79 "Principles of defence"; [Safety Tips][mu] | Someone who keeps moving is far harder to land a punch on, and the feet are what carry a boxer in and out of range. When a way out opens, run to a safe place with people in it. |
| Dodge right (`dodge_right`) | Moving sideways, not straight back, takes you out of an attack's path so you can get away | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 79 "The importance of lateral movement"; [Staying Safe][hpd], "When Physical Defense is Necessary" | Defences built on footwork should step sideways off the path of the attack instead of always moving in straight lines. Getting away, not winning a fight, is the aim. |
| Overhead strike (`overhead_strike`) | An arm raised over your head shields you from a blow coming down from above | [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Roof Block" | A forearm lifted level with the forehead covers the head against a strike from the front or from overhead. |
| Spear thrust (`spear_thrust`) | Two open hands out front keep someone at arm's length while you back away | [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Safety Stance"; [Safety Tips][mu] | Hands up with open palms, at least an arm's length from the other person, backing away. Keep your distance and tell the person to get back. |
| Stake thrust (`stake_thrust`) | Use your body weight, not just arm strength, to break a grip and get free | [Self Defense handout][asu], "Wrist Hold" and "Leverage your weight"; [Staying Safe][hpd], "When Physical Defense is Necessary" | Drop into a strong stance and lean in to break a hold; using your weight works whatever your size. Shift your weight to break free of a grab. |
| Uppercut (`uppercut`) | Up close a blow can come up from below, out of sight, so keep your chin down and hands up | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 78 "The rear hand uppercut" and p. 67 "Guard" | The uppercut belongs to close-quarter work and, like the hook, arrives from outside the defender's field of vision. A lifted chin is left exposed; the guard keeps the chin down and the hands up. |
| Chain punch (`chain_punch`) | Straight is quicker than a swing, so hands kept in front of you can hold off a rush | [What is Wing Chun?][iwca], "Centreline Theory"; [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 73 "The straight lead hand" | A straight line is the shortest path, so a punch down the middle arrives sooner than a curved one. A straight hand from the front holds up someone rushing in. |
| Double palm (`double_palm`) | A firm push with both open hands opens a gap, and a gap is your chance to leave | [Basic Attack Defense][ova], "Braced Choke Defense"; [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Safety Stance" | Once a hold is broken, shove the person away to make room, because room to move is what lets you get away. Hands up with open palms, an arm's length away, backing off. |
| Hammer fist (`hammer_fist`) | The padded bottom of your fist is safer for your hand than knuckles if you must break free | [Safer Strikes][pm] | Bare-knuckle punches put the hand at risk of injury. A strike with the bottom of the closed fist lands on the soft, fleshy part of the hand instead. |
| Knife hand (`knife_hand`) | A sweep of your open hand across and out knocks a reaching arm aside before it lands | [Fundamentals][sk], "Shuto-uke"; [Self Defense handout][asu], "Front and Back Choke Holds" | The open hand starts above the opposite shoulder and is thrust outward to meet and catch an arm that is striking. Swinging one arm across breaks a hold. |
| Reverse punch (`reverse_punch`) | Knees a little bent and back straight give you a steady base, so you stay on your feet | [The Mechanics of the Reverse Punch][yk]; [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 72 "Principles of punching" | The stance is the foundation: knees slightly bent to lower the body and steady it, back straight, shoulders relaxed. Every punch is driven from a good base. |
| Rising palm (`rising_palm`) | The heel of your open hand, pushed up and in, can loosen a grip so you can get away | [Self Defense handout][asu], "Nose"; [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Palm Heel Strike"; [Safer Strikes][pm] | Up close, the heel of the palm driven upward with your weight behind it makes someone loosen their grip. Fingers back, thumb tucked in. A palm strike is unlikely to break the hand. |
| Thunderbolt (`thunderbolt`) | A downward strike gets its force from gravity and your body weight, not arm strength. | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 72 "Principles of punching" | Same passage as the cross: the body drives a strike from the floor through the hips, and an "arm punch" is listed as an error. |
| Trident sweep (`trident_sweep`) | Sweeping across your body moves a grab or a reaching arm off your center line. | [Fundamentals][sk], "Shuto-uke"; [Self Defense handout][asu], "Front and Back Choke Holds" | Same passages as the knife hand: an arm swept outward meets and moves aside an arm that is reaching in. |
| Aegis push (`aegis_push`) | An open-palm push makes space to get away and is kinder to your hands than a fist. | [Basic Attack Defense][ova], "Braced Choke Defense"; [Safer Strikes][pm] | Same passages as the double palm and the hammer fist: shove the person away to make room, and bare knuckles put the hand at risk. |
| Swift dash (`swift_dash`) | One quick step off the line gets you out of the way faster than backing straight up. | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 79 "Principles of defence"; [Safety Tips][mu] | Same passages as the dodges: someone who keeps moving is far harder to reach, and the feet are what carry you out of range. |
| Moon arrow (`moon_arrow`) | A long guard arm keeps your distance while you set your feet and pick your moment. | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 73 "The straight lead hand" | Same passage as the jab: the front hand held out measures distance and holds up someone rushing in. |
| Sun burst (`sun_burst`) | Bursting both arms out from your chest breaks a front grab and opens space to leave. | [Self Defense handout][asu], "Front and Back Choke Holds"; [Basic Attack Defense][ova], "Braced Choke Defense" | Same passages as the knife hand and the double palm: break the hold with the arms, then make room to get away. |
| Jump (`jump`) | Getting your feet off the line of a low sweep keeps you standing. | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 79 "Principles of defence" | Same passage as the dodges: the feet are what carry you out of the way. The source covers stepping, not hopping; the line applies the same idea to a low attack. |
| Front kick (`front_kick`) | A front kick keeps someone at leg length, further away than their hands can reach. | [England Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1][eb], p. 73 "The straight lead hand" | Same passage as the jab: a straight limb out in front measures distance and holds up someone rushing in. The source covers the hand, not a kick; the line applies the same idea with the longer reach of a leg. |
| Knee (`knee`) | Held close with no room to swing an arm, a knee can still make the space to get away. | [Basic Attack Defense][ova], "Braced Choke Defense"; [Physical Self-Defense Strategies][aaf], "Safety Stance" | Same passages as the double palm: once held, make room, because room to move is what lets you get away. The passages cited cover a shove with the hands, not a knee; the line applies the same idea when the arms are pinned. |

## Notes

- Sources 2, 4 and 5 are written for people already under attack, and sources 6 and 7 for police officers, so they also describe strikes and the parts of the body to aim at. None of that is used here. We took only their points about keeping distance, covering the head, shifting body weight, protecting your own hand, breaking a grip and getting away, and wrote them in our own words.
- The spear thrust, overhead strike and stake thrust are stage moves with a spear and a stake. Their lines name the nearest real idea: hands out to hold distance, an arm raised to cover the head, and body weight against a grip. They do not claim the game's motion is a taught technique.
- The Karate and Kung Fu moves are strikes in the game. Their lines name the protective idea behind each one. The knife hand line describes the knife-hand block (shuto-uke), which makes the same sweep from the opposite shoulder as the strike.
- We found no governing body that publishes Wing Chun technique in words: the Ving Tsun Athletic Association's site has none. The chain punch line therefore rests on a Wing Chun school's page (source 10), backed by the boxing handbook on straight punches.
- This is a game, not a self-defense class. The lines point at ideas a real course teaches with a partner and an instructor.
